## Step 1: Exact-Match Web Search Engine Audit

Your first line of defense is an exhaustive exact-match search on global search engines like Google, Bing, and DuckDuckGo. A casual search without query operators is insufficient; you must force the search engine to return exact keyword sequences.

### How to Execute Exact Searches:
1. Wrap your proposed name in double quotes: `"Crimson Throne"`
2. Combine the quoted name with music-specific context operators:
   * `"Crimson Throne" band`
   * `"Crimson Throne" music`
   * `"Crimson Throne" album`
   * `"Crimson Throne" tour`
3. Inspect at least the first five pages of results, not merely the first page. Look for old band blogs, MySpace archives, festival line-up flyers, or local newspaper gig reviews.

### What to Look For:
* **Active Touring Bands:** If another group with your exact name is actively releasing songs, booking shows, or selling merchandise, stop immediately. Choosing the same name invites instant brand dilution and potential trademark infringement claims.
* **Defunct Legacy Acts:** If a band released a single demo tape in 1989 and vanished with no active streaming presence or registered trademarks, the risk may be substantially lower. However, consult an IP attorney if you are unsure whether legacy common-law trademark rights still apply.

## Step 2: Global Streaming Platform Verification

Streaming platforms are the primary discovery engine for modern music listeners. Having multiple artists share an identical artist name causes frequent catalog cross-contamination, where your debut track appears on another musician’s verified artist profile.

### Platforms You Must Check:
* **Spotify:** Search your exact name in the desktop and mobile Spotify apps. Check the "Artists" tab. If an artist with that name exists, check their monthly listeners and release dates. Have they released music within the past five years?
* **Apple Music:** Verify whether an existing artist profile exists in the Apple Music catalog.
* **Bandcamp:** Bandcamp is the global home of independent and underground music. Even if an obscure outfit never distributed to Spotify, they frequently host their discography on Bandcamp. A search on `bandcamp.com/search?q=[Name]` is indispensable.
* **SoundCloud:** Check for independent beatmakers, DJs, or acoustic acts sharing the moniker.
* **Discogs and Encyclopaedia Metallum:** For rock, metal, and niche genres, Discogs and Encyclopaedia Metallum maintain the world's most exhaustive historical discography databases. Searching these archives reveals obscure releases that standard web searches often overlook.

## Step 3: Social Media Handle Audit

Maintaining consistent, unified social media handles across major platforms makes promotional campaigns significantly more effective. When every flyer, album sleeve, and interview mentions `@YourBandName`, fans find you without friction.

### Check These Core Channels:
* **Instagram:** `instagram.com/[yourbandname]`
* **TikTok:** `tiktok.com/@[yourbandname]`
* **YouTube:** `youtube.com/@[yourbandname]`
* **X (formerly Twitter):** `x.com/[yourbandname]`
* **Facebook:** `facebook.com/[yourbandname]`

### Handling Taken Handles:
In modern music marketing, finding clean exact-match handles for all five platforms is increasingly rare. If your exact name is claimed by an inactive personal account, do not despair. Established bands routinely use standard, professional modifiers:
* `weare[bandname]` (e.g., `@wearecrimsonthrone`)
* `[bandname]music` (e.g., `@crimsonthronemusic`)
* `[bandname]band` (e.g., `@crimsonthroneband`)
* `[bandname]official` (e.g., `@crimsonthroneofficial`)

As long as you maintain the same modifier consistently across Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube, your branding remains clear and unified.

## Step 4: Domain Name Registration Check

While social media channels are vital for day-to-day engagement, owning your dedicated primary domain name remains the only digital asset you truly own and control. An official website gives your project an authoritative central home for press kits (EPKs), direct-to-fan merchandise stores, tour dates, and booking inquiries.

### Domain Guidelines:
1. **The `.com` Gold Standard:** Always check if the exact `.com` version is available. A `.com` address carries universal trust and effortless recall.
2. **Acceptable Alternatives:** If the `.com` is taken by a domain squatter asking exorbitant prices, consider modern creative alternatives:
   * `[bandname]music.com`
   * `[bandname]band.com`
   * Country code top-level domains (ccTLDs) such as `.co.uk`, `.ca`, or `.de` if your band is geographically focused.
3. Use a reputable domain registrar (such as Namecheap, Porkbun, or Google Cloud Domains) to check real-time availability without falling victim to registrar price gouging.

## Step 5: Official Trademark Registry Searches

This is the step that amateur musicians frequently skip, and the one that causes the most catastrophic legal complications later. Even if an artist has zero Spotify streams, if they have an active registered trademark for live musical performances in your territory, they possess superior legal rights to that commercial name.

### Where to Search (Free Public Registries):
* **United States:** The United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) offers the free **TESS (Trademark Electronic Search System)** portal. Search your proposed name across live records.
* **United Kingdom:** The UK Intellectual Property Office (**UKIPO**) offers a searchable trade mark register.
* **European Union:** The European Union Intellectual Property Office (**EUIPO**) maintains the **eSearch plus** database covering all EU member states.
* **International (WIPO):** The World Intellectual Property Organization operates the **Global Brand Database**, aggregating registered marks across dozens of international jurisdictions.

### The Key Nice Trademark Classes for Musicians:
When searching these trademark databases, pay particular attention to two primary international trademark classifications:
* **Class 041 (Entertainment Services):** Covers live musical performances, concert tours, and public presentations.
* **Class 009 (Sound Recordings & Media):** Covers pre-recorded musical media, vinyl records, digital audio files, downloadable musical sound recordings, and music video media.

If an entity holds an active, registered trademark in Class 041 or Class 009 for an identical or confusingly similar name within your territory, choosing that name presents high legal exposure.

## Step 6: The "Likelihood of Confusion" Standard

One common misconception among independent artists is believing that changing a single letter or altering spelling (such as replacing an "s" with a "z", or replacing "and" with an ampersand) bypasses trademark protection.

In trademark law, the legal test is not whether two names are 100% character-for-character identical; the test is **"Likelihood of Confusion"**. If an ordinary consumer purchasing music or attending a concert could reasonably confuse your band with an existing registered act due to phonetic similarity, identical translation, or similar commercial marketplace presence, infringement may still occur.

* [ ] **Google Exact Search:** No active touring musical acts share the exact phrase.

* [ ] **Underground Archives:** Checked Discogs and Encyclopaedia Metallum for legacy releases.
* [ ] **Social Media Handles:** Clean handles or consistent modifiers are claimable.
* [ ] **Web Domain:** An authoritative domain name is available for registration.
* [ ] **Government Trademark Databases:** Searched USPTO, UKIPO, or EUIPO in Class 041 and Class 009.
