## 4. Competitor-by-competitor detail

### #1 — omnicalculator.com/construction/rebar (AS 84)

- **H1:** Rebar Calculator. **Headings:** What is rebar? → Rebar sizes → How to use the rebar calculator → Rebar cost example → FAQ (4) → Related calculators.
- **Calculator:** slab length and width; rebar-to-rebar (grid) spacing; edge-to-grid spacing; at the supplier: price per metre, single bar length, price per bar. **Outputs:** grid length, grid width, total rebar length, number of pieces, total cost. Slab only; no bar size input, no weight, no lap, no waste.
- **Content:** formulas for grid size, total length, pieces (rounded up), cost, and bar volume × density (no density value given); bar-size table #2–#18 with soft-metric equivalents; list of true metric sizes.
- **Worked example:** 6 × 4 m slab, 40 cm spacing, 8 cm edge spacing, 6 m bars at $2/m → 125.68 m of steel, **19 pieces**, $252.
- **Error found (checked):** 125.68 m ÷ 6 m = 20.9 → **21 pieces**, not 19. Their own $252 equals 21 bars × 6 m × $2/m, so the cost uses 21 while the piece count says 19.
- **FAQs:** How to calculate rebar cost? · How to calculate rebar weight? · Do I need rebar for my patio? · Should I weld rebar?
- **Trust:** creator plus two reviewers (one with a civil-engineering background), editorial policy; no date, no sources.
- **Lesson for us:** good structure, but the most authoritative page in the SERP has a wrong number and no weight output. A correct, tested quantity **and** weight tool beats it on usefulness.

### #4 — procore.com/library/calculators/rebar-calculator (AS 60)

- **Title:** Rebar Calculator | Procore. Construction-software content marketing.
- **Calculator:** length, width, grid spacing, edge clearance, rebar-to-rebar spacing, rebar length — each with a unit dropdown (in, ft, yd, cm, m). Outputs were not visible in the capture.
- **Content (~500–600 words):** what rebar is; 3 steps (measure area and depth, choose bar size, count pieces). Practical guidance: #3–#5 for residential slabs, #4 for driveways and patios, #6+ for commercial; typical spacing 18–24 in.
- **Weak points:** the count method (length ÷ spacing × width ÷ spacing) skips the extra end bar and mixes up “count of intersections” with bars; calls bar size “gauge”; “grid spacing” vs “rebar-rebar spacing” is confusing; no weight, lap, waste or cost; no FAQ, no sources.
- **Trust:** named author, “Last updated Jul 11, 2024”.
- **Lesson for us:** users want **“which bar size and what spacing?”** guidance — but it must be framed as typical values plus “follow your plans/code”, never as structural design.

## 5. Patterns

- **Titles:** “Rebar Calculator” (2), “Rebar Weight Calculator” (2). Nobody targets “Reinforcement Steel Calculator” directly.
- **Intents:** slab quantity (Omni, Procore) vs. steel weight (Midland, Lemon, Heaton, probably Civil-Eng-Calc).
- **What nobody has:** lap length, waste %, footing/wall/column shapes, a weight-per-foot/metre table, a grid diagram, US + metric bar sizes in the calculator itself, or cited standards.
- **Numbers competitors mention (verify from primary standards before use):**
  - US bars #3–#8 commonly used in residential/commercial work (Procore).
  - Typical slab spacing 18–24 in (Procore).
  - Metric sizes 8, 10, 12, 16, 20, 25, 32, 40 mm (Midland).
  - Weight = bar volume × steel density (Omni, density not stated).
  - UK grade 500 N/mm², BS 4449:2005 (Lemon product copy).

## 7. Gaps nobody fills — our opportunities

1. **Quantity + weight in one tool.** Bars, metres/feet and kg/lb together — every competitor does only one.
2. **Bar size selector with both US # sizes and metric mm** — inside the calculator, not just in a table.
3. **Correct maths, tested.** Omni's example is wrong (19 vs 21 pieces); Procore's method skips the end bar. Our counts must include the end bars and round pieces up, with unit tests.
4. **Lap / overlap allowance** — no competitor has it. Let users enter lap length (e.g. as a multiple of bar diameter or a fixed length) and add laps when a run is longer than one stock bar.
5. **Waste %** — none have it.
6. **Shapes beyond a slab** — footing (longitudinal bars + optional stirrups), wall (vertical + horizontal), square/round column (vertical bars + ties). Start with slab + footing + wall; add columns later.
7. **Multiple items in one job** — slab + footings + walls summed by bar size (same multi-item pattern as our Concrete and Drywall calculators).
8. **Weight-per-foot/metre table** for every bar size — nobody publishes one, yet “rebar weight per foot” style questions drive this SERP.
9. **Grid diagram** showing spacing, edge cover and lap — no competitor has any diagram.
10. **Free, instant, no lead form** — beats Lemon's quote gate.
11. **Exact-match title** “Reinforcement Steel Calculator” — unused by everyone.
12. **Cited standards** — none of the ranking pages cite any. Cite the bar-size/weight standard (e.g. ASTM A615 for US bars, BS 4449 for UK) and state clearly that the tool estimates quantities, not structural design.

## 8. Blueprint for the KKCTools page

### 8.1 SEO

- **URL:** `/rebar-calculator/` (already planned in the keyword file)
- **Title (≤ 53 chars so it fits with “ | KKC Tools”):** `Rebar Calculator – Reinforcement Steel Bars & Weight`
- **H1:** `Reinforcement Steel Calculator: Rebar Quantity & Weight`
- **Meta:** Free rebar and reinforcement steel calculator for slabs, footings and walls. Get bar count, total length, laps and weight in lb or kg for US # or metric bars.
- Use “reinforcement steel calculator” in the H1 and first paragraph; “rebar calculator” in the title and an H2.

### 8.2 Calculator spec

| Area | Spec |
|---|---|
| Items | Slab/grid · strip footing · wall (v1); column later. Several items in one job |
| Inputs | Length, width/height, spacing each way, edge cover, bar size per direction, quantity; ft+in and m/cm with value conversion on unit switch |
| Bar sizes | US #3–#8 (optionally #9–#11) and metric 8–32 mm; weight per unit length from a cited standard |
| Stock length | 20 ft / 40 ft or 6 m / 12 m; pieces rounded up |
| Laps | Editable lap length (default as a multiple of bar diameter, labelled “check your plans”) added when a run exceeds stock length |
| Waste | Presets 0/5/10/15% |
| Outputs | Bars per direction, total length per bar size, pieces to buy, weight per size and total (lb/kg, tons/tonnes), optional cost per piece or per weight |
| Diagram | Live grid diagram (spacing, cover, laps) per item — same labelled-SVG approach as the Drywall/Concrete pages |
| UX | Copy / share link / print, mobile sticky result bar, dark mode, no network calls |

### 8.3 Content outline (≈ 2,000–2,500 words, original)

1. Intro (exact keyword) · 2. How to use · 3. How rebar quantity is calculated (with grid diagram, end-bar rule) · 4. Worked examples (slab, footing, wall, metric slab) · 5. Rebar sizes and weight per foot/metre table (US + metric) · 6. Typical bar sizes and spacing by project (framed as typical, follow plans/code) · 7. Laps and concrete cover explained · 8. Rebar vs wire mesh · 9. Common mistakes · 10. FAQ (10–12) · 11. Related tools (Concrete Calculator, Concrete Slab Cost, Cinder Block) · 12. Sources & disclaimer + last-updated date.

### 8.4 Before building — checks

- Get bar weights and nominal diameters from the primary standards (ASTM A615/A706 for US bars; BS 4449 or the metric formula based on 7,850 kg/m³ steel density) — log a `TODO(owner)` if a primary copy is not accessible.
- Lap and cover defaults must be presented as editable examples with a “follow your engineer's drawings / local code” note — no structural advice.
- Unit tests for every bar count, piece count and weight shown in examples and FAQ.
- Re-check the two unreachable competitors (#2, #7) manually in a browser.
