### #3 — easycalculation.com/area/pipe-volume-calculator.php (AS 48)

- **Title:** Volume of cylindrical pipe or tube Calculator with inner, outer radius
- Inputs: outer radius, inner radius, height (no units). Output: “cylindrical volume”. Formula V = πh(R² − r²). ~110 words.
- **Weak points:** radius not diameter (people measure diameter); no units; no capacity; filed under “Area”.

### #5 — calculatevolume.org (AS 10)

- **Title:** Volume Calculator — Free, Any Unit, Formula Shown
- Main tool is a box with per-field units and outputs in ft³, m³, L, US gal, UK gal; formula with numbers filled in; copy result. 13-shape formula table includes tube V = π(R² − r²)h; links to a separate pipe page.
- **FAQs (9):** how to calculate volume · formula · convert to gallons/litres · mix feet and inches · volume vs capacity · irregular objects · liquid volume · cubic volume · body volume.
- **Lesson:** AS 10 ranks on a **clean, verifiable output style** (formula with your numbers + all liquid units). Copy that approach for pipes.

### #6 — visual-paradigm.com tube volume calculator (AS 62)

- Inputs: outer diameter d1, inner diameter d2, length l. Formula V = π(d1² − d2²)/4 × l. Example: 10 cm OD, 6 cm ID, 3 cm long → 150.8 cm³ (checked correct). ~200 words, Chinese page shell.
- **Errors:** example calls the inner diameter “outer diameter d2”; no units.
- **Lesson:** this is the only page that matches “tube volume” exactly with diameters — and it's thin. Easy to beat.

### #7 — fourmationsales.com (AS 16)

- **Lorem ipsum** placeholder text, no tool, one outbound link to another site's calculator. Ranks #7 — proof the SERP is weak.

### #8 — eguruchela.com (AS 22)

- Outer radius, inner radius, height; no units; empty “Step-by-step solution” heading; typos. ~90 words.

### #9 — calculatorsoup.com volume calculator (AS 80)

- 11 solid shapes; units are display-only; total volume only. Author Edward Furey, updated Oct 2026. **No hollow cylinder.**

### #10 — horusvalley.com/en/tools/pipe-volume-calculator/ (AS 9) — the only real capacity tool

- **Title:** Pipe Volume Calculator – Liters & Cubic Meters
- Inputs: inside diameter (mm), length (m). Outputs: litres, m³, **litres per metre**, water weight (~1 kg/L). Dynamic diagram with PNG download.
- Content: worked example 100 mm × 10 m = 78.54 L (7.854 L/m, checked correct); **inside vs outside diameter** section; calculation notes.
- **Weak points:** metric only; no gallons, no inches; no nominal sizes; repeats the “use ID” warning ~4 times.
- **Lesson:** **capacity per metre/foot** and the **ID vs OD vs nominal** explanation are what pipe users need. Add imperial and pipe presets and we beat it.

## 5. Patterns

- **Formulas in use (all standard geometry):**
  - Capacity: V = π × (ID ÷ 2)² × L
  - Tube wall: V = π × (OD² − ID²) ÷ 4 × L = π × L × (R² − r²)
  - Conversions: 1 US gal = 231 in³ (exact) = 3.785411784 L · 1 imp gal = 4.54609 L (exact) · 1 ft³ = 7.48052 US gal · 1 L = 1,000 cm³ · water ≈ 1 kg/L (≈ 8.34 lb/US gal)
- **Nobody offers:** nominal pipe sizes, wall thickness input, square tubes, material weight, multiple runs, gallons per foot table, or a pipe-specific FAQ.
- **Trust is weak everywhere:** 0 of 8 cite a source; 3 show a date.

## 7. Gaps nobody fills — our opportunities

1. **Both modes in one tool:** *Liquid capacity* (ID) and *Tube material* (OD + ID or OD + wall). Nobody does both.
2. **Pipe size presets:** NPS ½"–12" with Schedule 40/80, copper type K/L/M, PEX, PVC, metric (mm) sizes — prefill ID/OD from cited standards. **0 of 8** have this.
3. **Wall thickness input** — ID = OD − 2 × wall (nobody accepts wall thickness).
4. **All liquid units:** US gal, imperial gal, litres, ft³, in³, m³, cm³ — plus **per foot and per metre**.
5. **Several runs** (e.g. 40 ft of ¾" + 12 ft of 1") → system total. Nobody does it.
6. **Weight:** water weight (or other liquid by density) and tube material weight (steel, stainless, copper, aluminium, PVC — editable density).
7. **Square / rectangular tube** mode.
8. **Gallons-per-foot / litres-per-metre table** for common sizes, generated by the engine.
9. **Real FAQ** — no competitor has a pipe FAQ.
10. **Trust:** date, sources (standards), formula shown with the user's numbers (CalculateVolume.org style).
