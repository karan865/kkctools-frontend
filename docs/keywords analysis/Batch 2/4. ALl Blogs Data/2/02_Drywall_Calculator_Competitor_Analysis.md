## 4. Competitor-by-competitor detail

### #1 — youtube.com (video, not a page)

Not a calculator tool; a video result. Counted as an **open slot** — no on-page content to beat, just a format difference. A well-built interactive tool is a reasonable alternative result for a "calculator" query.

### #2 — homedepot.com/c/project_calculators (AS 99) — blocked (403)

Could not be read directly. From the URL path and prior knowledge of Home Depot's calculator hub pattern (seen on other keywords in this research, e.g. its drywall-calculator path showed up for `figuring drywall` in earlier batches), this is almost certainly a **general directory of project calculators**, not a dedicated, deep drywall tool. It ranks on Home Depot's domain authority, not on drywall-specific content — a strong **but likely shallow** competitor for the exact query.

### #3 — turn2engineering.com/calculators/drywall-calculator (AS 22)

- **Title:** Drywall Calculator | Sheet & Cost Estimator · **H1:** Drywall Calculator
- **The deepest page in the SERP.** 18+ H2 sections: how-to-use, inputs/outputs reference, formulas (walls-and-ceiling vs walls-only vs cost), a step list, result interpretation, an input-quality checklist, a worked example, a sheet-size/reference table, a waste-factor table, a unit-conversion section, a comparison of calculator vs manual takeoff vs full estimate, common mistakes, troubleshooting, assumptions/sources, related calculators, a **glossary**, and FAQ.
- **Calculator:** room dimensions or total area toggle; include-ceiling toggle; identical-room multiplier; sheet size 4×8/4×10/4×12/4×14/4×16 (misses 4×9); waste %; doors/windows count+dimensions; a 4-level finish-level input whose effect on quantities is never shown; outside-corner bead length; cost inputs for sheets, compound, tape, screws, corner bead; U.S./metric unit presets; Auto/0/1/2-decimal precision; copy result, share, reset; a responsive "Visual Check" diagram.
- **Verified math:** the quick-estimate table's 12×12 room figures (14 sheets walls-only, 19 with ceiling) check out against the stated formula.
- **Weak points:** **mud/tape/screw quantities are never actually calculated** — only their *cost* fields exist, with no visible formula; finish-level input has no visible effect; multi-room support is identical-rooms-only (no list of different rooms); no save/print/PDF; no author or publish/update date despite the heavy technical content; the page is long and somewhat repetitive (the "How to Use" section partially duplicates the on-page table of contents).
- **Lesson for us:** this is the page to beat on **content depth and structure**. Its biggest hole — no real mud/tape/screw math — is exactly where we should be stronger.

### #4 — timothystoolbox.com/.../drywall-materials-calculator (AS 24)

- **Title:** Drywall Materials Calculator & Estimator Tool
- **Calculator:** a single input (total square feet of drywall), a "Materials Needed" output table (Wallboard Nails, Drywall Screws, Joint Tape) whose values were not visible in the capture (likely script-rendered). Stated assumptions: nail spacing 7" (ceiling) / 8" (wall), halved for adhesive/nail-on; screw spacing 12" (ceiling) / 16" (wall).
- **Content:** ~150 words, no formulas, no FAQ, no worked example. **No joint-compound output at all**, despite "materials calculator" in the title.
- **Trust:** published Sep 2024; no author; e-commerce trust badges ("5-Star," "251 Verified Reviews") rather than content authority.
- **Weak points:** only one input (no board size, ceiling/wall split, or waste factor); no mud estimate; label inconsistency ("Wal-Board" vs "Wallboard").
- **Lesson for us:** ranks on domain authority (AS 24) with almost no content — not a model to copy, but proof that even a thin page can hold a top-5 spot here, which should make a genuinely complete tool rank well.

### #5 — danthedrywallman.com/drywall-mud-calculator/ (AS 8)

- **Title:** Drywall Mud Calculator
- **Calculator:** coverage area input, a panel-size dropdown (4×8 through 4×16, with a **duplicate 4×12 entry** and sizes listed out of numeric order). Outputs: total panels, ready-mixed joint compound (lb), lightweight ready-mix compound (gal), setting-type compound (lb), lightweight setting-type compound (lb), nails, screws, joint tape (ft), **primer (gal)**.
- **Content:** two short paragraphs comparing three compound types; no formulas, no worked example, no FAQ (~150–200 words).
- **Weak points:** extremely thin explanatory content; no waste allowance; no metric; data-quality issues (duplicate/out-of-order sizes, a "Calulator" typo elsewhere on the site); no author, credentials or date (just "© 2013–2026").
- **Lesson for us:** the **widest compound-type breakdown** (ready-mix vs lightweight vs setting-type, regular vs lightweight) and a **primer output** are both things no other competitor includes — worth adding as an advanced/optional section.

### #6 — toolwick.com/drywall-calculator/ (AS 9)

- **Title:** Free Drywall Calculator · **H1:** Drywall Calculator
- **Headings:** embed widget → how-to-use → why this matters → how it's calculated → worked example → tips & mistakes → FAQ (6) → related tools → more calculators.
- **Calculator:** room length/width (1–500 ft), ceiling height (4–30 ft), sheet size dropdown (4×8/4×9/4×10/4×12/4×14/4×16 — the **only competitor with 4×9**), include-ceiling toggle, doors & windows as one combined sq-ft deduction, waste shown fixed at 10%, material cost per sheet, labor cost per sheet (labor has no visible output). Stated rules: screws ≈ 1/sq ft, tape ≈ 0.4 linear ft/sq ft, compound ≈ 0.0112 gal/sq ft.
- **Content:** ~1,000–1,100 words; one 9-step worked example (14×12 ft bedroom) whose arithmetic checks out; 6 FAQs; a wall-elevation + ceiling-plan diagram; a free iframe embed code.
- **Weak points:** imperial only; no save/print/share/multi-room; cost covers sheets only (screws/tape/compound not priced); no board-thickness choice despite discussing it in the tips; constants are explicitly called "planning averages, not manufacturer specifications" with no source; labor input is a dead end.
- **Lesson for us:** good, concise worked-example structure and the **only page with an embeddable widget** — a distribution idea worth considering later, not for v1.

### #7 — boqestimations.com/drywall-calculator (AS 7)

- **Title:** Drywall Calculator | 4x8 & 4x12 Sheet & Mud Estimator – BOQ Estimations · **H1:** Drywall & Plasterboard Calculator Online Tool
- **Explicitly targets both US ("drywall") and UK ("plasterboard") terminology**, with a 6-row terminology comparison table.
- **Calculator:** total room perimeter, wall height, include-ceiling dropdown. Preview output example: 842 sq ft total, 27× 4×8 sheets, 18× 4×12 sheets, 3× 4.5-gal mud buckets. No waste input/output, no tape or screw output despite being mentioned in copy, no openings deduction.
- **Content:** ~500–700 words; a terminology table; a generic "Bid Delivery Estimator" widget (not drywall-specific) with a 25%-off promo; 4 FAQs, one of which (waste factor) answers about **aggregate and concrete, not drywall** — a content mismatch.
- **Trust:** claims "Accuracy Guarantee: Up to 98%" with no stated method; physical Phoenix, AZ address; no author.
- **Weak points:** thin tool (no waste, no tape/screw math, no mud-rate basis); sales-first page structure; unsourced accuracy claim; mismatched FAQ content.
- **Lesson for us:** the **US-vs-UK terminology table** is a smart, cheap way to capture `plasterboard calculator` searches without a separate page — worth copying the *idea* (not the content) if UK/global traffic matters to us.

### #8 — exayard.com/tools/drywall/material-calculator (AS 19) — only partially captured

- **H1:** Free Drywall Material Calculator. Only ~70 words of marketing copy were visible ("Love this tool? Try the full platform," sign-up/demo CTAs). The intro claims the tool covers boards, joint compound, tape, screws and corner bead with "industry-standard coverage rates," but no actual inputs, outputs, formulas or content were visible — the real calculator likely sits behind their app sign-up flow.
- **Lesson for us:** a page that is mostly a lead-generation funnel for a paid platform, with almost no free, crawlable tool content. This is one of the weakest competitors in the SERP from a content-depth standpoint, despite AS 19.

### #9 — drywallcalculators.org ("DryCalc Pro") (AS 7)

- **Title:** Drywall Calculator, Cost and Materials | DryCalc Pro · **H1:** Free Drywall Calculator
- **Exact-match domain, and the second-most feature-rich tool in the SERP** after Turn2Engineering.
- **Calculator:** feet/meters toggle; **5 tabs** — Sheets, Cost, Mud & Tape, Screws, Weight:
  - *Sheets:* length/width/ceiling height, include-ceiling checkbox, sheet size (4×8/9/10/12), door count (21 sq ft each) and window count (15 sq ft each, **neither editable**), waste % (default 10%), outputs incl. "recommended purchase" rounded to a two-sheet bundle.
  - *Cost:* sheets, price/sheet, labor $/sq ft, total and per-sq-ft cost.
  - *Mud & Tape:* sheets, coats (1–3), corner bead option+length → compound (buckets+lb), tape (ft+rolls), corner-bead pieces. Stated rate: ≈0.053 five-gallon buckets per sheet per coat.
  - *Screws:* sheets, stud spacing (16"/24" OC), sheet size → screw count + boxes. Stated rate: ≈32 screws per 4×8 sheet at 16" OC, +10% allowance, 200 screws/box.
  - *Weight:* sheets, drywall type (standard/lightweight/fire-rated/moisture-resistant), sheet size → weight per sheet, total weight (lb/kg), a pickup-truck payload note.
  - **7 built-in room presets** (small bedroom, standard bedroom, master bedroom, living room, bathroom, 1-car garage, basement).
  - A sticky summary card (sheets, cost, compound, screws, weight) with print/copy.
- **Content:** ~3,000–3,500 words; one worked example (12×12 room, 8 ft ceiling → ~17 sheets with 10% waste, verified); sheet-size and drywall-type comparison tables; a 3-step guide; 2025 cost-range discussion; 6 pro tips; a **10-question FAQ** (the largest in the SERP).
- **Weak points:** no sourcing for any rate (0.053 buckets, 32 screws, 200/box); door/window deductions are fixed, not editable; no sloped-ceiling/soffit support though mentioned; no save/share/multi-room; **date inconsistency** (© 2025 footer vs. a linked "Drywall Cost Guide 2026"); heavy keyword repetition ("drywall calculator," "drywall sheet calculator," "drywall cost estimator") that reads as SEO-driven rather than natural; no schema confirmed despite the large FAQ.
- **Lesson for us:** the **tabbed Sheets/Cost/Mud & Tape/Screws/Weight structure** and **room presets** are the strongest UX ideas in the whole SERP. This is the main competitor to out-build.

### #10 — arcsite.com/utility/drywall-calculator (AS 38)

- **H1:** Drywall Calculator. Part of a large SaaS company's "20 utility calculators" content-marketing program (ArcSite sells pro estimating software).
- **Calculator:** room length/width/ceiling height (ft+in), sheet size radio (4×8/4×12/4×16), a "Calculate" button. **No output was visible in the capture** — possibly JS-rendered and missed, or genuinely thin. Waste is described in prose ("allows for an additional percentage of waste," 5–10% tip) but is not a field. Joint compound/tape/screws are *mentioned* in text but have no visible fields or formulas.
- **Content:** ~900 unique words, but the page **duplicates an entire H2/H3 "How to Use Pro Drywall Estimation Software" block twice** — a cleanup bug that hurts both readability and likely SEO. Only 3 FAQs, including "What is a drywall calculator?" and "Can this calculator estimate material cost?" (answered vaguely).
- **Trust:** no author; heavy trust marketing (21,000+ reviews across app stores/G2/Capterra, ISO 27001/SOC 2 badges) — brand trust, not content trust.
- **Weak points:** calculator appears to produce no visible result; no real formulas; no board-type/thickness input despite discussing thickness; imperial only; no door/window deduction; duplicated content block; the page functions mainly as a lead funnel to ArcSite's paid software.
- **Lesson for us:** ranks almost entirely on **domain authority + heavy internal linking** (~20 other `/utility/` calculators), not on this page's own content quality — another sign that a genuinely useful free tool can outrank it.

## 7. Gaps nobody fills — our opportunities

1. **No page correctly calculates mud, tape and screws from one consistent, sourced formula.** Three competitors attempt it (Dan the Drywall Man, Toolwick, DryCalc Pro) and their rates for screws and compound actually disagree with each other. → Build all three from one documented, sourced rate set (screw spacing per code/manufacturer spec, tape per linear foot of seam, compound per coat), and show the formula on the page the way Turn2Engineering shows its sheet-count formula.
2. **No page handles different (non-identical) rooms in one job.** Turn2Engineering only multiplies one room type; everyone else is single-room. → Let users add Room 1 (bedroom), Room 2 (bathroom ceiling only), etc., and sum sheets/mud/tape/screws across all of them — same multi-item pattern as our live Concrete Calculator.
3. **No page covers both US and metric sheet sizes properly** (only Turn2Engineering and DryCalc Pro offer any metric option, and neither lists a metric sheet size like 1200×2400 mm / 1200×2700 mm). → Add real metric board sizes, not just a unit toggle on US dimensions.

5. **Fixed, non-editable door/window deductions** (DryCalc Pro hard-codes 21/15 sq ft). → Make door and window count *and* size fully editable, like Turn2Engineering and Toolwick already do — but also calculate mud/tape/screws off the *same* net area, which none of them do consistently.
