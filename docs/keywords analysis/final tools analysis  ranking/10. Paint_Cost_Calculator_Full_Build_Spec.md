## 5. Mathematical Calculation Specifications

### 5.1 Wall Surface Area Formula
$$\text{Room Perimeter (linear ft)} = 2 \times (\text{Length} + \text{Width})$$
$$\text{Gross Wall Area (sq ft)} = \text{Perimeter} \times \text{Ceiling Height}$$
$$\text{Openings Deduction (sq ft)} = (\text{Doors} \times 21) + (\text{Windows} \times 15) + \text{Custom Deductions}$$
$$\text{Net Wall Area (sq ft)} = \max(0, \text{Gross Wall Area} - \text{Openings Deduction})$$

### 5.2 Ceiling Area (Optional)
$$\text{Ceiling Area (sq ft)} = \text{Length} \times \text{Width}$$
$$\text{Total Surface Area per Coat} = \text{Net Wall Area} + (\text{Ceiling Area if toggled})$$

### 5.3 Paint Volume Calculation
$$\text{Total Paintable Sq Ft} = \text{Total Surface Area per Coat} \times \text{Coats}$$
$$\text{Exact Gallons Needed} = \frac{\text{Total Paintable Sq Ft}}{\text{Coverage Rate (sq ft/gal)}}$$

### 5.4 Container Purchasing Breakdown
Since paint is commercially retailed in **1-Gallon cans**, **1-Quart cans** ($0.25 \text{ gal}$), and **5-Gallon buckets**:
- If $\text{Exact Gallons} \le 1.0$:
  - If $\le 0.25 \text{ gal} \rightarrow 1 \text{ Quart}$
  - If $\le 0.50 \text{ gal} \rightarrow 2 \text{ Quarts}$
  - If $> 0.50 \text{ gal} \rightarrow 1 \text{ Gallon}$ (buying 3 quarts is often more expensive than 1 full gallon)
- For larger jobs:
  $$\text{Full Gallons to Buy} = \lceil \text{Exact Gallons} \rceil$$
  *(If $\ge 5 \text{ gallons}$, display 5-gallon contractor bucket option for bulk cost savings).*

### 5.5 Cost Calculation
$$\text{Paint Cost} = \text{Full Gallons to Buy} \times \text{Price Per Gallon}$$
$$\text{Primer Cost} = \text{Full Primer Gallons} \times \text{Price Per Primer Gallon}$$
$$\text{Total DIY Cost} = \text{Paint Cost} + \text{Primer Cost} + \text{Supplies Cost}$$

## How to Calculate Paint for a Room (Step-by-Step)

Estimating paint quantities with pinpoint accuracy requires four straightforward measurements:

### Step 1: Measure Room Perimeter and Ceiling Height
Measure the length and width of the room in feet. Add the lengths of all four walls together to find the room's total perimeter:
$$\text{Perimeter (ft)} = 2 \times (\text{Length} + \text{Width})$$
Next, multiply the perimeter by the ceiling height to determine the gross wall area:
$$\text{Gross Wall Area (sq ft)} = \text{Perimeter} \times \text{Ceiling Height}$$
*Example:* A standard 14 ft × 12 ft bedroom has a perimeter of $2 \times (14 + 12) = 52 \text{ linear ft}$. With an 8-foot ceiling, the gross wall surface area is $52 \times 8 = 416 \text{ sq ft}$.

### Step 2: Deduct Doors, Windows, and Large Openings
You do not paint over doorways, glass windows, or fireplace mantels. Deduct these unpainted openings from your gross wall area using standard architectural sizing:
- **Standard Interior Door:** 21 sq ft ($3 \text{ ft} \times 7 \text{ ft}$)
- **Standard Window:** 15 sq ft ($3 \text{ ft} \times 5 \text{ ft}$)
- **Large Picture Window / Sliding Glass Door:** 40 to 60 sq ft

$$\text{Net Wall Area (sq ft)} = \text{Gross Wall Area} - \text{Total Opening Deductions}$$
*Example:* If the bedroom has one door (21 sq ft) and two windows ($2 \times 15 = 30 \text{ sq ft}$), total deductions equal $51 \text{ sq ft}$. The net wall surface area to paint is $416 - 51 = 365 \text{ sq ft}$.

### Step 3: Account for the Ceiling (Optional)
If you plan to repaint the ceiling with fresh ceiling white flat paint, calculate the ceiling area simply as length times width:
$$\text{Ceiling Area (sq ft)} = \text{Length} \times \text{Width} = 14 \times 12 = 168 \text{ sq ft}$$

### Step 4: Multiply by the Number of Coats and Apply Coverage Rates
Standard interior wall paint typically covers **350 to 400 square feet per gallon** on smooth, primed drywall. Because professional results require **two full coats**, multiply your net square footage by 2, then divide by the coverage rating:
$$\text{Total Sq Ft to Cover} = 365 \text{ sq ft} \times 2 \text{ coats} = 730 \text{ sq ft}$$
$$\text{Gallons Needed} = \frac{730 \text{ sq ft}}{350 \text{ sq ft/gal}} = 2.08 \text{ gallons}$$
In this scenario, purchasing **2 full gallons** plus a quart (or 3 gallons to keep a touch-up reserve) ensures flawless coverage.

## How Much Does a Gallon of Paint Cover? (The 350–400 Sq Ft Rule)

The universal rule of thumb in the painting industry is that **one gallon of quality interior paint covers approximately 350 to 400 square feet** on smooth, non-porous surfaces. However, wall texture and porosity can significantly alter your real-world coverage:

| Wall Surface Type | Spread Rate per Gallon | Best Suited Paint Finish | Notes |
|---|:---:|---|---|
| **Smooth Drywall (Primed)** | **380 – 400 sq ft** | Eggshell, Satin, Matte | Standard newly painted or primed modern drywall |
| **Light Texture (Orange Peel)** | **340 – 360 sq ft** | Eggshell, Satin | Slight surface ripples increase surface area by 10–15% |
| **Heavy Texture (Knockdown / Stucco)** | **280 – 320 sq ft** | Flat, Matte | Deep crevices absorb paint rapidly |
| **Unprimed Fresh Drywall / Plaster** | **250 – 300 sq ft** | PVA Drywall Primer | Highly porous paper and mud suck in moisture thirsty |
| **Rough Masonry / Brick / CMU** | **200 – 250 sq ft** | Masonry Primer / Acrylic | Heavy porous texture; requires dense nap roller |

## Paint Sheen Guide: Which Finish Should You Choose?

Selecting the proper paint sheen is just as important as choosing the right color:

1. **Flat / Matte (0–5% gloss):**
   - *Best For:* Ceilings, adult bedrooms, low-traffic formal living rooms.
   - *Pros:* Hides wall imperfections, dents, and drywall joints exceptionally well. Non-reflective.
   - *Cons:* Difficult to wipe clean; scrubbing can cause burnishing (shiny spots).
2. **Eggshell (10–25% gloss):**
   - *Best For:* Living rooms, dining rooms, bedrooms, hallways.
   - *Pros:* Soft, warm glow resembling an eggshell. Moderately washable, durable, and hides minor drywall flaws. The #1 most popular interior wall finish in America.
3. **Satin (25–35% gloss):**
   - *Best For:* High-traffic hallways, kids' playrooms, kitchens, and laundry rooms.
   - *Pros:* High moisture resistance and easy to wipe clean with a damp sponge.
4. **Semi-Gloss (35–60% gloss):**
   - *Best For:* Bathrooms, baseboards, window casings, interior doors, and kitchen cabinets.
   - *Pros:* Exceptional resistance to water, grease, and repeated scrubbing.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How much paint do I need for a 12x12 room?
For a standard 12 ft × 12 ft room with an 8-foot ceiling, total gross wall area is 384 sq ft. Subtracting one standard door (21 sq ft) and two windows (30 sq ft) leaves approximately 333 net sq ft. Because paint covers roughly 350 to 400 sq ft per gallon, you will need exactly 1 gallon for a single coat, or 2 full gallons for the recommended two coats.

### How many gallons of paint do I need for 1,000 square feet?
For 1,000 square feet of wall surface area, you will need approximately 2.5 to 2.85 gallons of paint per coat (based on standard coverage of 350 to 400 sq ft per gallon). For the industry-standard two coats, plan on purchasing 5 to 6 gallons of paint.

### How much does a gallon of interior paint cover?
One standard US gallon of interior latex or acrylic paint covers approximately 350 to 400 square feet on smooth, primed drywall. On unprimed drywall, textured walls, or porous surfaces (like rough plaster or masonry), coverage drops to 250 to 300 square feet per gallon.

### Do I really need two coats of paint?
Yes, in nearly all interior painting projects, two coats are strongly recommended. A single coat rarely achieves full color opacity, leaves roller lap marks, and fails to provide uniform durability. Even self-priming "paint-and-primer-in-one" formulas require two coats when transitioning between contrasting colors.

### How much does it cost to paint a room yourself vs hiring a painter?
DIY painting for an average 12x12 room costs between $70 and $180 total for two gallons of mid-to-premium paint plus basic supplies (roller, tape, drop cloth). In contrast, hiring a professional painting contractor typically costs between $350 and $900 for the same room, including labor, wall prep, patching, and two coats.

### When is a dedicated primer coat required?
A separate primer coat is essential when: (1) painting brand new unpainted drywall or fresh skim coats, (2) transitioning from a dark paint color to a light color, (3) painting over repaired drywall patches, stains, or smoke damage, or (4) applying water-based latex paint over older oil-based enamel.
