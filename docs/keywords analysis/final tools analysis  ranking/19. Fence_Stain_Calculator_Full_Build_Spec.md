### Step-by-Step Calculation Guide
## How Much Stain for a Fence? (Coverage & Gallons Formula)

To accurately calculate **how much stain for a fence** before driving to the home improvement center:

### 1. Measure Total Linear Footage
Measure the total running length of the fence in feet:
$$\text{Total Linear Feet} = \text{Perimeter Run (ft)}$$
*Example:* A standard suburban backyard fence measuring 150 linear feet.

### 2. Multiply by Fence Height to Get Single-Side Area
Multiply the length by the fence height in feet:
$$\text{Single-Side Area} = \text{Linear Feet} \times \text{Height (ft)}$$
*Example:* For a 6-foot-tall fence: $150\text{ ft} \times 6\text{ ft} = 900\text{ square feet}$.

### 3. Multiply by 2 for Both Sides
Unless your fence directly borders a neighbor's property line that you cannot legally or physically access, you will need to stain both the interior and exterior faces:
$$\text{Both-Sides Area} = 900\text{ sq ft} \times 2 = 1,800\text{ square feet}$$

### 4. Apply Your Fence Picket Style Multiplier
Different fencing styles possess varying amounts of exposed board edges and overlaps:
- **Standard Flat Privacy:** Multiply by $1.0\times$ ($1,800\text{ sq ft}$).
- **Board-on-Board (Overlapping):** Multiply by $1.30\times$ ($1,800 \times 1.30 = 2,340\text{ sq ft}$).
- **Shadowbox (Alternating Pickets):** Multiply by $1.25\times$ ($1,800 \times 1.25 = 2,250\text{ sq ft}$).
- **Split Rail (3-Rail):** Multiply by $0.40\times$ ($1,800 \times 0.40 = 720\text{ sq ft}$).

### 5. Factor in Wood Porosity and Grain Absorption
- **Rough-Sawn Cedar or Unfinished Pine:** Absorbs stain heavily, yielding roughly **175 sq ft per gallon**.
- **Aged, Dry, Weathered Wood:** Highly porous, yielding roughly **150 sq ft per gallon**.
- **Smooth Planed or Previously Sealed Wood:** Yields **200 to 250 sq ft per gallon**.

### 6. Calculate First Coat and Second Coat Gallons
- **First Coat:** Divide total effective area by wood absorption rate ($1,800 / 175 = 10.3\text{ gallons}$).
- **Second Coat:** The second coat absorbs less liquid because the wood pores are partially sealed. Increase coverage by 30% ($1,800 / (175 \times 1.30) = 7.9\text{ gallons}$).
- **Total Gallons (2 Coats):** $10.3 + 7.9 = 18.2\text{ gallons}$.

### 7. Add 10% to 15% for Sprayer Overspray and Drips
If applying with an airless paint sprayer on an outdoor windy day, up to 15% of the stain can be lost to airborne overspray. When using brushes and rollers, add 10%:
$$\text{Total Gallons to Order} = 18.2 \times 1.10 = \mathbf{20.0\text{ Gallons}}$$

### Transparent vs. Semi-Transparent vs. Solid Stain
## Choosing the Right Stain: Transparent vs. Semi-Transparent vs. Solid

Selecting the proper stain formulation impacts durability, aesthetic appearance, and recoat frequency:

1. **Clear / Transparent Water Repellents (1 to 2 Year Life):**
   - Contains minimal pigment. Highlights 100% of the natural wood grain and knots.
   - Provides minimal UV sun protection. Best for high-end Western Red Cedar or Redwood that you are willing to re-seal every 12 to 18 months.
2. **Semi-Transparent Penetrating Oil Stains (3 to 5 Year Life) — THE BEST ALL-AROUND CHOICE:**
   - Contains finely ground iron oxide pigments that tint the wood while letting natural grain patterns show through.
   - Soaks deep into wood pores without forming a surface film. Never peels, chips, or blisters; to re-apply in 4 years, simply clean the fence and spray a fresh coat directly over top.
3. **Solid Color Acrylic Stains (5 to 7 Year Life):**
   - Looks like flat exterior house paint, completely masking the wood grain while showing texture.
   - Provides maximum UV and moisture defense. Best for older, discolored, or mismatched weathered fences. However, solid stains form a film that can eventually crack or peel, requiring scraping before recoating.

### Pro Contractor Staining Tips
## 5 Essential Pro Tips for Staining a Wood Fence

1. **Test Wood Moisture Before Staining (The Water Drop Test):** Never stain new pressure-treated lumber immediately after installation. Pressure-treated wood is injected with liquid preservatives and takes 2 to 4 months of warm, dry weather to dry out. Splash water droplets onto the wood: if the water beads up into droplets, the wood is too wet to absorb stain; if the water immediately absorbs into the wood, it is ready to stain.
2. **Wash the Fence Before Staining:** Gray weathered wood is coated in oxidized, dead wood cellulose fibers. Spray the fence with an oxygenated wood bleach or exterior wood cleaner, let it sit for 15 minutes, and gently pressure wash at low pressure (under 1,500 PSI) with a wide 40-degree green nozzle tip. Let the fence dry for at least 48 hours before applying oil stain.
3. **Always Back-Brush When Spraying:** While using an airless paint sprayer speeds up application significantly, never just spray and walk away. Have a partner follow immediately behind the sprayer with a 4-inch wide synthetic bristle stain brush to "back-brush" the wet stain deep into the wood pores, vertical picket seams, and nail holes.
4. **Stain Vertically from Top to Bottom:** Always work along 2 to 3 pickets at a time from the top of the fence down to the bottom. Never stop midway down a picket to take a break; always stain pickets to completion to maintain a "wet edge" and prevent dark overlapping lap marks.
5. **Protect Grass and Concrete Borders:** Penetrating oil stain will permanently discolor concrete walkways and kill lawn grass. Lay heavy canvas drop cloths or sheets of cardboard along the base of the fence line, and wet down adjacent shrubs with water from a garden hose before spraying so any stray droplets rinse off easily.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many gallons of stain do I need for a 150-foot fence?
A standard 6-foot-tall, 150-foot wooden privacy fence has 900 sq ft per side (1,800 sq ft total for both sides). On rough-sawn cedar absorbing stain at 175 sq ft per gallon, one coat on both sides requires approximately **10 to 11 gallons**. For two coats with a 10% buffer, order approximately **20 gallons** (or four 5-gallon contractor pails).

### How many square feet does 1 gallon of fence stain cover?
On smooth, previously sealed wood, 1 gallon covers approximately **200 to 250 square feet**. On rough-sawn, unsealed cedar or aged weathered pine, coverage drops to **150 to 175 square feet per gallon** due to heavy wood grain absorption.

### Does a board-on-board fence take more stain?
Yes. A board-on-board fence features overlapping vertical pickets, creating approximately **30% more surface area and edge exposure** than a flat privacy fence. Always multiply flat fence square footage by 1.30 when estimating stain for board-on-board designs.

### Should I apply one coat or two coats of stain?
For semi-transparent or transparent penetrating oil stains on new or bare wood, **2 coats provide maximum UV protection and weatherproofing**. For solid color acrylic stains or routine maintenance refreshing, 1 uniform coat is usually sufficient.

### How often should a wood fence be stained?
A wooden fence should typically be restained every **3 to 5 years** when using a quality semi-transparent penetrating oil stain, or every **1 to 2 years** for clear sealers. Solid color acrylic stains can last **5 to 7 years** before requiring maintenance.

### Can I spray fence stain with a pump garden sprayer?
Yes, but only if you use a thin, penetrating oil-based stain (such as Ready Seal or TWP). Thick water-based acrylic stains will clog the small plastic nozzle of a pump sprayer. For thick stains, use an airless paint sprayer (such as a Graco Magnum X5) or a 3/4-inch nap paint roller with back-brushing.
