## H2: Quick Answer: How Many Cubic Yards in a Ton of Rock?
On average, **1 US short ton of crushed rock contains approximately 0.71 to 0.74 cubic yards**. Conversely, **1 cubic yard of standard crushed stone or gravel weighs between 1.35 and 1.50 tons** (2,700 to 3,000 pounds).

Because rock varieties have different porosities and mineral densities, the exact conversion depends on the material you order:
- **#57 Crushed Stone:** 1.40 tons/yd³ (0.71 yd³ per ton, 2,800 lbs/yd³)

## H2: How to Calculate Stone and Rock Needed (Step-by-Step Formula)
Quarries and aggregate supply yards sell bulk rock by weight (tons), but you measure your landscaping beds in feet and inches. Translating your jobsite dimensions into tons involves six straightforward steps:

1. **Measure Area in Square Feet:** Multiply length by width in feet (Area = Length × Width). For circular areas, measure diameter, calculate radius (diameter ÷ 2), and multiply Area = 3.14159 × radius × radius.
2. **Convert Depth to Feet:** Divide your depth in inches by 12: Depth in Feet = Depth in Inches ÷ 12. For instance, a 3-inch depth equals 3 ÷ 12 = 0.25 feet.
3. **Calculate Cubic Feet and Cubic Yards:** Volume in Cubic Feet = Area × Depth in Feet. Volume in Cubic Yards = Volume in Cubic Feet ÷ 27. For example, a 30 ft × 6 ft border (180 sq ft) at 3 inches deep: 180 × 0.25 = 45 cu ft. Dividing 45 ÷ 27 gives 1.67 cubic yards.
4. **Apply a Waste and Settling Allowance:** Subgrades have uneven dips, and aggregate settles under foot and tire traffic. Always add 5% for garden borders or 10% for driveways and trenches. 1.67 gross cubic yards becomes 1.83 cubic yards with 10% waste.
5. **Multiply by Stone Density:** Total Tons = Gross Cubic Yards × Material Density (tons per yard). At 1.35 tons per yard for river rock: 1.67 net yards × 1.35 = 2.25 net tons, or 1.83 gross yards × 1.35 = 2.48 gross tons (order 2.5 tons).

## H2: Landscape Rock Calculator: How Deep Should Your Stone Be?

### River Rock Calculator: Coverage & Depth
When using a **river rock calculator** for decorative borders or dry creek beds, size dictates depth. For medium 1- to 3-inch river cobble, spread a minimum depth of **3 inches** (or twice the diameter of the largest rock). A 2-inch layer of 3-inch stones leaves noticeable bare voids. At 3 inches deep, 1 ton of river rock covers approximately 80 square feet.

### Crushed Stone Calculator: #57 vs Crusher Run
- **#57 Stone (Clean angular 1/2" to 1"):** Ideal for French drains, backfilling retaining walls, and clean decorative driveways. Recommended depth is **3 to 4 inches**. It has roughly 40% void space for rapid water drainage.
- **Crusher Run / Dense Grade (3/4" down to stone dust):** Ideal for structural driveway sub-bases and paver patio foundations. Recommended depth is **4 to 6 inches**, compacted in 2-inch lifts. The dust particles pack tightly between rocks, forming a rock-hard unshifting base.
- **Pea Gravel (3/8" round pebbles):** For patio floors and pathways, keep depth to **2 to 2.5 inches**. Any deeper and foot traffic will sink awkwardly into the loose stones.

## H2: Aggregate Calculator: How Contractors Estimate Stone
Professional excavators and landscape contractors use practical rules of thumb when ordering aggregate from commercial quarries:
- **Certified Scale Tickets:** Quarries weigh haul trucks on certified platform scales (gross weight minus empty tare weight). You pay for the scale weight, which includes natural stockpile moisture. After heavy rain, damp aggregate weighs 5% to 8% more.
- **Subgrade Settling Allowance:** Raw dirt subgrade yields when heavy stone is spread. Excavators routinely round up to the nearest half-ton or full ton to prevent shortages that halt skid-steer crews.
- **Gradation Void Ratios:** Clean single-size stone (#57) holds large voids, requiring less weight per yard than blended aggregates (crusher run or road base) where stone dust fills every pocket.

## H2: 5 Pro Tips for Laying Landscape Stone
1. **Always Lay Heavy Commercial Geotextile Fabric First:** Never spread rock directly onto raw subsoil. Without fabric, stones sink into mud while dirt worms migrate up. Use needle-punched non-woven landscape fabric secured with heavy 6-inch steel landscape staples every 3 feet.
2. **Install Rigid Deep Edging:** Rounded river stones and pea gravel migrate into grass lawns during leaf blowing or heavy rains. Install commercial poly, steel, or stone edging standing 1/2 inch to 1 inch above the final stone surface.
3. **Lay Plywood Over Driveways Before Delivery:** Dump truck wheel loads and tailgate dumps can scratch or crack concrete driveways. Lay two 4 ft × 8 ft sheets of 3/4-inch plywood on your driveway for the driver to dump the aggregate onto.
4. **Hose Off Quarry Dust After Spreading:** Freshly delivered bulk stone is coated with fine pulverized rock dust from crushing plants. Thoroughly wash your newly installed rock beds with a garden hose to reveal brilliant natural mineral colors.
5. **Avoid Rounded River Rock on Slopes:** Round river cobble rolls under gravity like marbles. For hillside swales, ditch linings, or slopes steeper than 3:1, always specify angular crushed stone or heavy rip rap that mechanically locks into place.

## H2: Frequently Asked Questions
1. **How many cubic yards are in a ton of rock?** One US short ton of rock contains approximately 0.65 to 0.78 cubic yards, depending on the mineral density and gradation. For standard #57 crushed stone and pea gravel (density ~1.40 tons/yd³), 1 ton equals 0.71 cubic yards. For dense crusher run (~1.50 tons/yd³), 1 ton equals 0.67 cubic yards. For smooth river rock (~1.35 tons/yd³), 1 ton equals 0.74 cubic yards. Lightweight lava rock (~0.61 tons/yd³) yields roughly 1.64 cubic yards per ton. Conversely, 1 cubic yard of common crushed rock weighs between 1.35 and 1.50 tons.
2. **How do I calculate how much rock I need?** To calculate how much rock you need: (1) Measure your project length and width in feet, and determine desired depth in inches. (2) Multiply Area (sq ft) × Depth (in) ÷ 12 to find cubic feet. (3) Divide cubic feet by 27 to convert to cubic yards. (4) Add 5% to 10% for grade variations and settling. (5) Multiply gross cubic yards by the stone material density rating (typically 1.35 to 1.50 tons per cubic yard) to determine total short tons to order.
3. **How many square feet does 1 ton of rock cover?** One ton of typical crushed stone or river rock covers approximately 105 to 120 square feet at a 2-inch depth, 70 to 80 square feet at a 3-inch depth, and 52 to 60 square feet at a 4-inch depth. Because coverage depends on stone density, heavier materials like crusher run cover slightly less (~54 sq ft at 4"), while lighter river rock covers slightly more (~60 sq ft at 4").
4. **How many 50 lb bags of stone make a cubic yard?** It takes approximately 54 to 56 bags (50 lb each) of stone to equal one cubic yard of typical crushed rock or gravel. Since one cubic yard of standard aggregate weighs between 2,700 and 2,800 pounds, divide total weight by 50 to find bag count: 2,700 ÷ 50 = 54 bags, or 2,800 ÷ 50 = 56 bags.
5. **What is the difference between #57 stone and river rock?** #57 stone is machine-crushed, angular limestone or granite with sharp edges (roughly 1/2" to 1" in size) that lock loosely together while maintaining large open drainage voids, making it the industry standard for French drains, concrete aggregate, and sub-bases. River rock consists of naturally water-smoothed, rounded pebbles and cobbles (1" to 3"+), making it comfortable for walkways and visually attractive for decorative landscape beds, but unsuitable for driveways because round stones roll under vehicle tires.
6. **Is #57 stone or crusher run better for a driveway?** Crusher run (also called dense-graded aggregate or ABC stone) is superior for driveway foundations because it contains a blend of 3/4-inch crushed stone down to fine stone dust. When compacted, the stone dust fills all voids, creating a hard, cement-like interlocking base that resists rutting. #57 clean stone is commonly used as a clean decorative top dressing or for drainage courses, but lacks the binding fines needed for a firm, compacted roadway base.
7. **Why is my supplier's weight different from the calculator's result?** Quarries and aggregate supply yards bill by certified platform scale tickets, measuring actual gross weight on delivery trucks. Rainwater or high quarry stockpile moisture can increase material weight by 5% to 10% without adding usable volume. Additionally, natural mineral density variations from different quarry ledges mean actual scale weights may vary slightly from theoretical dry volume estimates.
8. **Can I put landscape rock directly on dirt?** We strongly advise against placing landscape rock directly onto raw soil. Over time, rainfall and foot traffic will cause heavy stones to sink into the mud while soil washes up into the rocks. Weeds will also rapidly take root. Always install a heavy-duty, professional-grade non-woven geotextile landscape fabric between the subgrade dirt and your stone layer to block weeds, allow water drainage, and keep your rock clean for years.
