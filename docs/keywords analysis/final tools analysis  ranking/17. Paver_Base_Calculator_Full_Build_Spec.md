### Header & Intro
# Free Paver Base Calculator: Estimate Tons & Cubic Yards of Crushed Stone

A stunning interlocking brick, stone, or concrete paver patio is an exquisite addition to any home, increasing usable outdoor living space and boosting property value. However, the long-term success of any paver installation is determined entirely by what lies underneath. When patios sink, pavers wobble, or walkways heave after freezing winter temperatures, the blame almost never rests on the pavers themselves—it is caused by an inadequate, poorly compacted crushed stone base.

Calculating how much base aggregate to order is tricky because of **compaction shrinkage**. When loose crushed gravel (such as crusher run, road base, or #21A limestone) is vibrated with a heavy gas-powered plate tamper, air voids collapse and the stone particles interlock tightly, causing the physical volume to shrink by **15% to 20%**. If you order only the raw excavated volume, your finished base will be significantly too thin!

Our contractor-grade **paver base calculator** (also known as a **crushed stone paver base calculator** or **paver base tons calculator**) solves this challenge by automatically incorporating mechanical compaction shrinkage, providing exact cubic yards, tons, and retail bag counts.

### Step-by-Step Calculation Guide
## How Much Paver Base Do I Need? (Formula & Compaction Loss)

To manually calculate your paver base foundation, follow this 5-step engineering formula:

### 1. Measure the Excavation Surface Area
Always measure the length and width of the patio in feet. **Crucial Pro Rule:** Excavate your base **6 inches wider on all sides** than your planned paver footprint to support the edge restraints (snap-edge or concrete toe). For a 20 ft × 15 ft patio, excavate a 21 ft × 16 ft area:
$$\text{Excavation Area} = 21\text{ ft} \times 16\text{ ft} = 336\text{ square feet}$$

### 2. Determine Finished Base Depth
Convert your required base depth from inches into feet by dividing by 12:
- Standard Patio / Walkway: 4 inches ($4 / 12 = 0.333\text{ ft}$)
- Driveway / Hot Tub: 6 to 8 inches ($8 / 12 = 0.667\text{ ft}$)

### 3. Calculate Finished Compacted Volume
Multiply the excavation area by the finished base depth:
$$\text{Compacted Cubic Feet} = 336\text{ sq ft} \times 0.333\text{ ft} = 111.9\text{ cubic feet}$$

### 4. Multiply by the 15% to 20% Compaction Loss Factor
To ensure you have enough stone after plate compaction, multiply by 1.15 (for 15% compaction) or 1.20 (for 20% compaction on clay soils):
$$\text{Loose Volume to Order} = 111.9\text{ cu ft} \times 1.15 = 128.7\text{ cubic feet}$$

### 5. Convert to Bulk Cubic Yards and US Short Tons
Divide loose cubic feet by 27 to obtain cubic yards, then multiply by standard crusher run density (1.40 tons per cubic yard):
$$\text{Cubic Yards to Order} = \frac{128.7}{27} = \mathbf{4.77\text{ Cubic Yards}}$$
$$\text{Weight in Tons} = 4.77\text{ cu yds} \times 1.40\text{ tons/yd³} = \mathbf{6.68\text{ US Short Tons}}$$
(Round up to order **7.0 tons** from your local quarry or landscape supplier).

### Material Comparison: What Goes Under Pavers?
## Crushed Stone vs. Paver Base: What Material is Best?

Homeowners often wonder: *"What exact material is paver base made of?"*

### 1. Dense-Graded Aggregate (Crusher Run / #21A Limestone / ABC Stone) — THE BEST CHOICE
- **Composition:** A blend of 3/4-inch jagged crushed stone mixed with smaller chips, grit, and fine limestone rock dust.
- **Why It Works:** When compacted, the small particles and stone dust fill every void between the larger stones. When vibrated with water, it sets like soft concrete, creating an unyielding, non-shifting subbase that resists frost heave.

### 2. Recycled Concrete Aggregate (Crushed Concrete Base) — EXCELLENT BUDGET CHOICE
- Clean, pulverized concrete sieved to 3/4-inch minus. Offers identical structural performance to crushed limestone at a 20% to 30% lower cost per ton.

### 3. Clean Washed Stone (#57 Stone without fines) — DRAINAGE ONLY
- While great for French drains, clean stone lacks the fines necessary to lock together tightly under plate compaction. Do not use clean stone for your main paver base unless building a specialized permeable paver system.

### Pro Contractor Compaction Tips
## 5 Essential Pro Tips for Compacting Paver Base

1. **Always Compact in 2-Inch to 3-Inch Lifts:** Never dump 6 inches of loose gravel into a hole and attempt to compact it all at once. Standard rental plate compactors (weighing 150 to 200 lbs) only produce enough compaction force to penetrate 2 to 3 inches deep. The bottom 3 inches will remain loose, causing major sinkholes within two years. Lay 2 inches of stone, compact thoroughly, then repeat.
2. **Moisten the Stone Before Compacting:** Dry stone generates excessive friction and creates clouds of dust. Spray the gravel with a garden hose until damp (like a wrung-out sponge). Water lubricates the stone particles, allowing them to slide tightly together into zero-void compaction.
3. **Overdig the Perimeter by 6 Inches:** If your patio is 15 feet wide, your crushed stone base must be 16 feet wide. Paver edge restraints require at least 6 inches of solid compacted base extending past the outside edge of the pavers to anchor 10-inch steel spikes securely.
4. **Lay Heavy Geotextile Fabric Under the Base:** On clay or silty soils, always lay a heavy woven or non-woven geotextile stabilization fabric directly over the raw subsoil before dumping gravel. This prevents sharp gravel from sinking into soft mud over time.
5. **Check Slope for Water Runoff (1 Inch per 8 Feet):** Never build a flat patio. Grade your compacted base to slope away from home foundations at a rate of 1 inch of downward fall for every 8 to 10 linear feet.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
## Frequently Asked Questions

### How deep should a paver base be?
For pedestrian walkways and residential patios, standard paver base depth is **4 inches of compacted crushed stone**. For heavy use areas such as hot tub pads, outdoor kitchens, or vehicular driveways, increase the base depth to **6 to 10 inches**.

### What is the best material to use for a paver base?
The industry standard paver base is **dense-graded aggregate** (commonly sold as crusher run, road base, #21A limestone, or recycled concrete). This blend of 3/4-inch crushed rock down to fine stone dust compacts into a rock-solid, impermeable foundation that will not rut or shift.

### Why do I need to add 15% to 20% for compaction?
When loose crushed stone is run over with a heavy gas-powered vibratory plate tamper, air voids collapse and the stone particles interlock tightly. This reduces the physical volume by 15% to 20%. Failing to order extra will leave your base significantly lower than planned.

### How many bags of paver base equal one cubic yard?
Standard retail bags of paver base contain 0.5 cubic feet (approx 50 lbs). Because one cubic yard contains 27 cubic feet, you need **54 retail bags** to equal one bulk cubic yard.

### Can I use sand as a paver base instead of gravel?
No. Never use sand as your primary base. Sand is fluid and easily washes away or shifts during heavy rain, which will cause your pavers to become wavy, uneven, and dangerous to walk on within months. Sand is strictly used as a thin 1-inch bedding layer on top of a solid 4-inch crushed stone foundation.

### How much does a ton of paver base cost?
At local commercial quarries and bulk landscape supply yards, bulk crusher run or #21A paver base typically costs between **$30 and $55 per ton**, plus a local delivery dump fee ($50 to $90). In bagged form at retail box stores, the same material costs approximately $160 to $220 per ton.
