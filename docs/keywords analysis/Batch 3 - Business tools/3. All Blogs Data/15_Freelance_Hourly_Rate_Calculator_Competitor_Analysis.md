**What this tells us:**
- **Two directions:** (a) *goal → rate* (Harvest, Pastel, Everhour, Memtime, Indy) and (b) *rate → take-home income* (Rize). Nobody does both cleanly.
- **The hard part is realistic billable hours** (non-billable admin/sales time, holidays, sick days) and **costs a salaried job covers for free** (tax, health insurance, retirement, paid leave). Tools handle these partially.
- **Output mismatches:** Everhour shows $65/hr × 960 h ≠ its own $91,200; Rize's “take-home” ignores income tax.

## 4. Competitor-by-competitor detail

### Harvest — freelance rate calculator
- Target income, working weeks, hours/week, billable % (60%), annual expenses → **break-even rate**, **recommended (+20%)**, billable hours/week, daily rate. Formula shown. **FAQs (8):** how to calculate · expenses to include · taxes to set aside · non-billable time · average US rate · how often to adjust · pricing models · multiple scenarios.
- **Weak points:** tax not modelled (just a buffer); personal costs mentioned but no input; unsourced average.

### Pastel — hourly rate calculator
- 11 itemised annual/monthly business cost fields (rent, travel, equipment, software, phone, insurance, legal, accounting, supplies, ads, other) → total costs, ideal rate, break-even rate. **No income/hours inputs visible**; no FAQ.
- **Lesson:** **itemised costs** help people not forget things.

### Rize — rate calculator (reverse)
- Hourly rate, billable hours/week, weeks/year, health insurance, retirement % → gross, after expenses, monthly take-home, effective $/hr; SE tax breakdown (15.3% × 92.35%). W-2 comparison email-gated.
- **FAQs (5):** set my rate · expenses · vs W-2 salary · track billable hours · average 2026 rate.
- **Errors:** “take-home” excludes income tax; effective-rate definitions conflict; unsourced rate tables.

### Everhour — freelance rate calculator
- Annual expenses, profit margin (30%), billable hours, non-billable buffer (20%) → ideal rate (blank), minimum rate $65, effective hours 960, revenue $91,200.
- **Error:** $65 × 960 = $62,400, not $91,200. Article example: ($101k income + $15k overhead + $22k benefits + $32k tax) ÷ 1,360 h = $125/hr vs ÷ 2,080 = $81.73.
- **FAQs (7):** how to calculate · billable hours to use · bill rate vs take-home · hourly vs project · biggest US tax number · (2 product FAQs).

### Indy — freelancer hourly pricing calculator (blog + Google Sheet)
- Overheads explained (health insurance, SE tax, accountant, software, paid leave, retirement); questions to ask (demand, competitors, lifestyle); formula $75,000 ÷ 52 ÷ 40 × 1.2 = $43.27; value-based pricing; negotiation; contract. Author, 2019/2023.
- **Weak points:** flat 1.2× overhead; 40 billable h/week unrealistic; calculator off-page.

### Memtime — freelance hourly rate calculator (DE)
- Target income (USD/EUR/GBP), days off, hours/day, billable hours/day, business costs, tracking method → hourly rate + “30 extra billable minutes/day = $X”. **FAQs (6):** how to calculate · how it works · hourly or fixed monthly · what freelancers charge · work hours · billable efficiency.
- **Weak points:** no formula/example; no tax/VAT/social contributions despite German focus.

## 5. Patterns

- **Maths:** available days = 52×5 − holidays − vacation − sick − training · billable hours = available days × hours/day × billable % · required revenue = target take-home ÷ (1 − effective tax %) + business costs + benefits (health, retirement, leave) · rate = required revenue ÷ billable hours · recommended = rate × (1 + profit/buffer %) · day rate = rate × billable hours/day.
- **Salary equivalent:** compare with employee salary + employer-paid benefits (link Hourly to Salary #4).

## 7. Gaps nobody fills — our opportunities

1. **Two-way tool:** target income → rate **and** rate → yearly revenue/take-home estimate.
2. **Realistic calendar:** holidays, vacation, sick, training days + billable % → billable hours (shown clearly).
3. **Itemised costs** (Pastel-style list) + benefits a job would pay (health, retirement, paid leave).
4. **Tax as a simple, editable effective rate** (no hard-coded national rules) with optional US SE-tax helper citing IRS year.
5. **Break-even, recommended (+margin), and day/week/project rate** outputs.
6. **Employee salary equivalent** (link Hourly to Salary #4).
7. **Currency + VAT note** (UK/EU freelancers add VAT on top; rates usually quoted ex-VAT).
8. **Correct, consistent outputs** (Everhour mismatch).
9. **No email gate; no product push.**
